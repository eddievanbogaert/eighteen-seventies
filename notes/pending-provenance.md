# Pending provenance

Material that has reached the repository but cannot yet be used, because where
it came from is unknown.

This file exists so that such material is neither quietly used nor quietly
lost. Nothing listed here may be cited, and nothing here has a `CLAIMS.md`
row. An item leaves this file in one of two directions: its provenance is
established and it moves to `/primary` with a row in `sources/RETRIEVED.md`,
or it is abandoned and the entry records that it was.

The distinction being enforced is the one in rule 2. A document is not
evidence because it looks like evidence. It is evidence because someone can
say where it is held, what it is a copy of, and who made the copy.

## Elisha Gray's caveat drawing — received 2026-11-13

**Status: unusable. Provenance unknown.**

**What arrived.** A GIF image, 164 KB, cached at
`sources/cache/gray-caveat-1876/caveat-drawing-ef38db33761131ad.gif`, sha256
prefix `ef38db33761131ad`. Supplied by the author, origin not stated.

**What the image shows.** A single drawing sheet, lettered at the head:

> ELISHA GRAY
> INSTRUMENTS FOR TRANSMITTING AND RECEIVING VOCAL SOUNDS TELEGRAPHICALLY
> CAVEAT FILED FEBRUARY 14TH 1876

Three figures. Fig. 1 and Fig. 3 show a speaker at a cone-shaped mouthpiece
and a listener at a receiver, connected by a line above a ground return.
Fig. 2 shows a vessel with a stem entering it from above. At the foot,
"WITNESSES." over two signatures, and "INVENTOR," over a signature reading
*Elisha Gray*. The witness signatures were not confidently legible.

**Why it cannot be used.** Everything about its provenance is missing. Not
which repository holds the original; not what the GIF is a reproduction *of* —
the caveat sheet itself, a printed facsimile in a book, a court exhibit, or a
later redrawing; not who made the reproduction, or when, or whether it was
retouched. A drawing of unknown origin that depicts what one would expect the
document to depict is exactly the kind of evidence this project's rules exist
to refuse. Its plausibility is not a point in its favour.

**What would make it usable.** Any one of:

- A repository and identifier — most likely the National Archives, which
  holds Patent Office records — with an image served from that repository.
- A published facsimile with its bibliographic details, so the reproduction
  can be cited as a reproduction and the publisher named as part of the
  provenance.
- A court record of the telephone litigation reproducing the caveat as an
  exhibit, cited as that exhibit.

**What it is wanted for.** Q12-2, competing priority claims, and Q12-3, the
allegation of irregularity. Both are gates for chapter 12. Note that the
drawing alone would not close them even with perfect provenance: the drawing
is not the caveat's text, and the dispute turns on what the text described and
when. The drawing would be a start, not an answer.

**A caution worth recording.** This is the item most likely to be used
carelessly, because it is visually striking, it is on the single most
contested point in the chapter, and it appears to show exactly what the
received story says it shows. That combination is a reason for more care, not
less.
