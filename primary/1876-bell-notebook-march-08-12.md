## Notebook page 38 — the close of the 8 March entry

Experiments 12 to 14 are not transcribed. The closing passage is, because it
sets out the arrangement that becomes the 9 March apparatus.

> (Thoughts.)
>
> It seems as if the sound from M (Fig 7, 8, 9, 10, 11) is loudest when the metallic surface B Fig 10 is largest and the vibrating surface in contact with the water smallest.
>
> Try the following arrangement. Fasten wire W to stretched membrane m.
>
> Noted March 8th by A.G.B.

## Notebook page 39 — 9 March 1876, in full

> March 9th 1876
>
> 1. The apparatus suggested yesterday was made and tried this afternoon.
>
> A membrane (m) Fig 1 — was stretched across the bottom of the box (B). A piece of cork (c) was [struck: hollowed] attached to the centre of the membrane (m) [struck: forming] a support for the wire W, which projected into the water in the glass vessel V.
>
> The brass ribbon R was immersed in the water also. Connections were made as in the diagram (Fig 1).
>
> Upon singing into the box B the pitch of the voice was clearly audible from S — which latter was placed in [struck: a different] another room. When Mr. Watson talked into the box — an indistinct mumbling was heard at S. [struck: resembling the confused] I could hear a confused muttering sound like speech but could not make out the sense. When Mr. Watson counted — I fancied I could perceive the articulations "one, two, three, four, five" — but this may have been fancy — as I knew beforehand what to expect. However that may be I am certain that the inflection of the voice was represented 1 2 3 4 5.
>
> Noted March 9th by A.G.B.

A second hand has initialled the foot of the page, `G.G.H.`

## Notebook pages 42–43 — 10 March, experiments 2 to 7

Experiments 2, 3, 5 and 6 are not transcribed. Two are, for what they say
about the apparatus.

> 4. The pipe P being of brass, and the wire W of platinum the arrangement constituted in reality a battery. [struck: The] black deposit formed upon W which had to be removed every minute or two.

> 7. A large number of experiments made to test the effect of varying the surface of W exposed to the liquid have convinced me that the amount of surface exposed at W has little or nothing to do with the effect. The sound proceeding from S was sensibly as loud when the mere point of W touched the water as when a large mass of metal (connected with W) was immersed in the water.

## Two observations about the documents

**The sequence is continuous and dated.** 8 March proposes an arrangement;
9 March builds it and gets pitch, inflection and a possible "one, two, three,
four, five" that Bell himself distrusts as "fancy"; 10 March builds an
improved instrument and gets a sentence understood through a closed door. The
notebook dates each step and Bell attests them in his own hand.

**Bell records his own doubt.** The 9 March entry discounts its most
suggestive result on the ground that he knew beforehand what to expect, and
the 10 March entry applies the same test in reverse — Watson reads an unknown
passage, and Bell reports failing to follow it. A chapter that quotes only the
successful sentence is quoting the least careful page of a careful record.

**The initials are unidentified.** `G.G.H.` and `M. G. H.` appear in hands
other than Bell's. Who they were is not established here and must not be
assumed. Logged as an open question against Q12-1.
