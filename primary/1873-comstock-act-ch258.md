# The act of 3 March 1873, ch. 258: title, section 1, placement

**Transcription only.** Commentary belongs in `/notes`.

## Provenance

- **Document:** *The Statutes at Large of the United States of America*,
  vol. 17, Forty-Second Congress, Session III, chapter CCLVIII, printed
  pages 598-600.
- **Copy transcribed from:** the Library of Congress digitisation of volume
  17 (`llsl-c42.pdf`), supplied by the author. Page images.
- **Form:** page images, read as images. Printed page = PDF page index + 41
  in the cached file, checked against the running heads of printed 598-600.
- **Transcribed by:** Claude (assistant editor), 2026-11-13.

**The act has no popular name in the statute book.** "Comstock Act" is a
later usage and appears nowhere on these pages. The chapter keeps the two
apart; see the drafting blocker in `chapters/08-comstock-act.md`.

## Heading, printed page 598

> March 3, 1873.
>
> CHAP. CCLVIII.—*An Act for the Suppression of Trade in, and Circulation of, obscene Literature and Articles of immoral Use.*

## What stands on the same page

Printed page 598 carries the end of chapter CCLVI, "An Act to authorize the
continued Employment of an Agent and Counsel of the United States", and the
whole of chapter CCLVII, on the face value of Texas and Pacific Railroad
bonds. Above those is the close of a post-roads act, listing roads from
Huntington to Wayne Court House, from Miller's Ferry to Fayetteville, from
Roncevert to Lewisburg, from Pruntytown to Meadland. Every one of them,
including chapter CCLVIII, is approved March 3, 1873.

That is recorded here because it is evidence about how the act passed and
not an impression of it: the statute sits in the undifferentiated run of
business at the end of a session, between a railroad's bonds and a board of
steam-vessel inspectors for the customs district of Michigan.

## Section 1, opening, printed page 598

> *Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the United States of America in Congress assembled,* That whoever, within the District of Columbia or any of the Territories of the United States, or other place within the exclusive jurisdiction of the United States, shall sell, or lend, or give away, or in any manner exhibit, or shall offer to sell, or to lend, or to give away, or in any manner to exhibit, or shall otherwise publish or offer to publish in any manner, or shall have in his possession, for any such purpose or purposes, any obscene book, pamphlet, paper, writing, advertisement, circular, print, picture, drawing or other representation, figure, or image on or of paper or other material, or any cast, instrument, or other article of an immoral nature, or any drug or medicine, or any article whatever, for the prevention of conception, or for causing unlawful abortion, or shall advertise the same for sale, or shall write or print, or cause to be written or printed, any card, circular, book, pamphlet, advertisement, or notice of any kind, stating when, where, how, or of whom, or

## Section 1, penalty, printed page 599

> shall be imprisoned at hard labor in the penitentiary for not less than six months nor more than five years for each offense, or fined not less than one hundred dollars nor more than two thousand dollars, with costs of court.

## Marginal notes to section 1, printed pages 598-599

The marginal notes are the printer's summary, not enacted text:

> Penalty for, in any place within the exclusive jurisdiction of the United States, selling or possessing obscene books, pictures, &c.;
>
> or drugs, &c., for preventing conception or causing abortion; or advertising or making the same.
>
> Imprisonment and fine.

## Notes on the transcription, not about history

- Section 1 is one sentence. It is transcribed here in two parts because the
  sentence runs across the page break at 598/599 and because the middle of
  it — the means-of-procurement clause — is continued at the top of 599 and
  is given in the companion file with section 2.
- "unlawful abortion" is the statute's phrase. The word "unlawful" is doing
  work and must not be dropped in quotation.
- The first part above ends where the page ends, mid-clause, with "of whom,
  or". No ellipsis is supplied, because the break is the page's and not an
  editorial choice.
