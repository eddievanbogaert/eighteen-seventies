# Grant's veto of Senate Bill 617, 22 April 1874

**Transcription only.** Commentary belongs in `/notes`.

## Provenance

- **Document:** Ulysses S. Grant's **autograph draft** of his veto message to
  the Senate of the United States, dated in the edition `[April 22, 1874]`,
  returning Senate Bill No. 617 without approval.

  The edition's source note at printed p. 75 reads: "ADf (bracketed material
  not in USG's hand; ellipses in original), DLC-USG, III. SED, 43-1-44; SMD,
  49-2-53, 386-89." ADf is the edition's siglum for an autograph draft. **This
  is therefore Grant's working draft and not the message as sent**, which that
  note locates in Senate Executive Document 43-1-44. The two have not been
  compared. Nothing here may be described as what Grant sent to the Senate.
- **Printed in:** *The Papers of Ulysses S. Grant, Volume 25: 1874*, ed. John
  Y. Simon (2003), pp. 73–75.
- **Copy transcribed from:** a PDF of that volume, 535 pages, hosted by
  Mississippi State University Scholars Junction. Supplied by the author,
  2026-11-13. 161,261,031 bytes, sha256 `6dfc07c3f5ed1111…`.
- **Form:** page images, read as images. The file carries an OCR text layer
  which was used **only** to locate the message; it is unreliable, rendering
  "of" as "ef", "pledges" as "pied ges" and "say" as "S<!)l".
- **Pagination:** printed page = PDF page − 30, checked at pp. 70–74.
- **Transcribed by:** Claude (assistant editor), 2026-11-13.

**Rights.** The volume's own front matter states that the material may be
protected by U.S. copyright. Two things are tangled here and should be kept
apart: **Grant's words are a United States government document of 1874 and
are not in copyright.** Simon's editorial apparatus — the headnotes, the
annotations, and the reconstruction of Grant's cancellations — is scholarly
work of 2003 and is. Quoting Grant from this edition is a different act from
reproducing the edition's apparatus, and the book should do the first and not
the second. Flagged for the author.

Grant's cancellations, as the edition shows them, are given `[struck: …]`.

Two conventions, from the edition's own source note, and the first of them
corrects an error in an earlier version of this file. **The ellipses `. . .`
are in Grant's original**, not editorial omissions: he was marking places
where quoted matter was to be inserted. **Square brackets mark material not in
Grant's hand.** So a bracketed word inside a quotation is somebody else's, and
quoting it as Grant's would be wrong.

>

## Observations about the document, not about history

**The bill has a number, and the number is in the message.** Senate Bill
No. 617. The edition dates the veto 22 April 1874 in brackets, which is the
editorial convention for a date supplied rather than written on the document.

**The deletions run one way.** Every cancellation visible in this passage
removes a hedge or a qualification. "If not in every Annual Message" becomes
"in every Annual Message". A long clause conceding that his first message was
the main one, and that nothing since had been inconsistent with it, is struck
out entirely, leaving the flat claim that the pledge appears in every annual
message and every inaugural address. "So large" is struck from a
characterisation of the majority that passed the bill.

A reader is watching a draft get firmer. Whether that is a man clarifying his
position or hardening it is not a question `/primary` answers.

**The argument is from pledge, not from theory.** What survives the revision
is a chain of citations — the 1869 annual message, an 1865 House resolution
carried 144 to 6, the first act of the Forty-First Congress of 18 March 1869
— each adduced as a promise already made. The message argues that resumption
was owed, not that inflation would fail. The economic reasoning in the first
paragraph above is secondary to it.

**Not yet read.** Printed p. 75, the remainder of the message. The edition's
annotations, which are the editor's work and carry their own rights position.
The House and Senate proceedings on the veto. Grant's remarks of 1 June 1874
at p. 116 and the draft annual message of 7 December 1874 at p. 272, both
cited by Calandro and both still unverified.
