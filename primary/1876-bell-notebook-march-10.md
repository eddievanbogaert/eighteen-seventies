## Three observations about the document, not about history

Recorded because a later reader would otherwise re-derive them. None is an
argument; each is a statement about what is on the page.

**The sentence as Bell wrote it.** The page reads "Mr. Watson — Come here — I
want to see you", with spaced dashes and a capitalised "Come". When Bell
records Watson repeating it back, he writes it lower-case: "come here". The
familiar modern forms — "Mr. Watson, come here, I want to see you", or the
variant ending "I want you" — are not what this page says. Any version used in
the book is checked against this transcription.

**The instrument is a liquid transmitter.** Fig. I shows a brass pipe and a
platinum wire standing in liquid. This is the variable-resistance method, the
method of claim 4 of patent 174,465, and the subject of the specification's
liquid passage transcribed in `1876-bell-patent-174465-undulatory.md`.

**The dates, in the order the documents give them.** The specification is
signed 20 January 1876; the application is filed 14 February 1876; the patent
is dated 7 March 1876; this entry is dated 10 March 1876. On the face of these
four documents, the patent issued three days before the instrument described
here was built and tried. What follows from that — if anything — is Q12-2 and
Q12-3, and is not decided in `/primary`.
