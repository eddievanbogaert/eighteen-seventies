## § 13 — the standard, printed p. 426

> SEC. 13. That the standard for both gold and silver coins of the United States shall be such that of one thousand parts by weight nine hundred shall be of pure metal and one hundred of alloy; and the alloy of the silver coins shall be of copper, and the alloy of the gold coins shall be of copper, or of copper and silver; but the silver shall in no case exceed one-tenth of the whole alloy.

## § 14 — the gold coins, printed p. 426

> SEC. 14. That the gold coins of the United States shall be a one-dollar piece, which, at the standard weight of twenty-five and eight-tenths grains, shall be the unit of value; a quarter-eagle, or two-and-a-half dollar piece; a three-dollar piece; a half-eagle, or five-dollar piece; an eagle, or ten-dollar piece; and a double eagle, or twenty-dollar piece. And the standard weight of the gold dollar shall be twenty-five and eight-tenths grains; of the quarter-eagle, or two-and-a-half dollar piece, sixty-four and a half grains; of the three-dollar piece, seventy-seven and four-tenths grains; of the half-eagle, or five-dollar piece, one hundred and twenty-nine grains; of the eagle, or ten-dollar piece, two hundred and fifty-eight grains; of the double-eagle, or twenty-dollar piece, five hundred and sixteen grains; which coins shall be a legal tender in all payments at
>

## § 67 — the short title and the repeal, printed p. 435

> SEC. 67. That this act shall be known as the "Coinage act of eighteen hundred and seventy-three;" and all other acts and parts of acts pertaining to the mints, assay-offices, and coinage of the United States inconsistent with the provisions of this act are hereby repealed: *Provided,* That this act shall not be construed to affect any act done, right accrued, or penalty incurred, under former acts, but every such right is hereby saved; and all suits and prosecutions for acts already done in violation of any former act or acts of Congress relating to the subjects embraced in this act may be begun or proceeded with in like manner as if this act had not been passed; and all penal clauses and provisions in existing laws relating to the subjects embraced in this act shall be deemed applicable thereto:
>
> [margin: This act to be known as coinage act, &c.]
> [margin: Other acts, &c., repealed; such repeal not to affect, &c.]

Francisco branch-mint building. It is not transcribed.

## Observations about the text, not about history

**"Shall be the unit of value" is in § 14.** The phrase sits inside a list of
gold coin denominations and weights, in a subordinate clause attached to the
one-dollar piece. It is not given a section of its own and carries no heading
beyond the marginal "Gold coins;".

**The two legal-tender provisions differ.** Gold coins under § 14 are "a legal
tender in all payments". Silver coins under § 15 are legal tender "for any
amount not exceeding five dollars in any one payment". The asymmetry is in the
enacted text.

**§ 67 closes the system.** It repeals prior acts and parts of acts pertaining
to the coinage that are inconsistent with this one. Taken with § 15, which
omits the standard silver dollar from the list of silver coins, and § 17,
which forbids issuing any coin not set forth in the act, the three provisions
together leave no route by which the standard silver dollar could be struck.

That last paragraph describes how the sections fit. It does not assert what
anyone intended, what anyone understood, or what followed. Those are Q07-2,
Q07-3 and Q07-4, and none of them is settled here.
