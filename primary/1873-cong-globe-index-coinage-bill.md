## The entry, index p. cxv, middle column

> Coinage, bill (H. R. No. 2934) revising and amending the laws relative to the mints, assay offices, and, of the United States.
>
> reported, 203; amendments reported, 363; read, 661; discussed, 668; passed with amendment, 674; conference, 868, 871; conference report, 1150; concurred in by Senate, 1150; by House, 1214; enrolled, 1282.

The comma before "of the United States" is as printed: the index inverts the
title so that "Coinage" leads, leaving the phrase broken where the catchword
was lifted out.

## A neighbouring entry, index p. cxv, left column

Transcribed because it is a different bill with a similar name and the two
must not be confused.

> Coin, bill (S. No. 1265) to punish and prevent the unlawful making, selling, or passing, or having in possession with intent to sell or issue, of any false, or token in the resemblance of any, of the United States—[By Mr. Stewart.]
>
> referred, 205; reported, 1040; discussed, 1420.

## What this establishes, and what it does not

**Establishes.** The measure that became the Coinage Act of 1873 was a House
bill, H. R. No. 2934. On the Senate side it was reported at Globe p. 203,
amendments were reported at 363, it was read at 661, discussed at 668, and
passed with amendment at 674. It then went to conference, noted at 868 and
871; the conference report appears at 1150 and was concurred in by the Senate
at 1150 and by the House at 1214; it was enrolled at 1282.

**Does not establish.** Nothing whatever about what was said. An index entry
records that a thing was discussed and on which page; it does not record the
discussion. Q07-2 asks who drafted the silver provisions and what the debates
addressed, and Q07-3 asks whether the demonetisation was understood by the men
who voted. Neither can be touched until the pages themselves are read.

The word "discussed" against a single page, 668, with passage six pages later,
is a fact about the index's own conventions and not yet a fact about the
Senate. How much of p. 668 the bill occupies, and whether discussion continued
on pages the index does not flag, is unknown until the page is seen.

## Access note

The index volume is addressed as
`congress.gov/cg/ll/llcg/108/{block}/{seq:04d}r{seq:03d}.jpeg`, where `block`
is the sequence number rounded down to a hundred. That pattern was confirmed
across the range and the index ends at roman 296.

The body of the Globe for this session is in a different volume. Its address
has not been found: neighbouring volume numbers return 404 under every label
form tried, no sequence after the index resolves, and congress.gov returns 403
to a script requesting the HTML pages that would show the viewer's own URLs,
while serving the JPEG images freely. One example URL of any arabic-numbered
Globe page for this session would be enough to derive the rest.
