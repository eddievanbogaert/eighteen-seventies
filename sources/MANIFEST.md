# SOURCE MANIFEST

Every source the book uses, with a stable `key` that `/research/CLAIMS.md`
cites. A source that is not in this table cannot be cited anywhere.

## Read this before using anything below

**These entries were seeded from an agent's recall and are pointers for
acquisition, not citations.** Author, title, and year all need checking against
the actual copy before any of it reaches a footnote. Rule 2 in `AGENTS.md`
applies to this file with no exception: the fact that a book is listed here is
not evidence that it says what you think it says, or that it exists in the
edition you assume.

Nothing carries `acquired: y` or `read: y` yet, because nothing has been
acquired or read. Do not flip either flag on anyone else's behalf.

Deliberately absent:

- **Page numbers, ISBNs, publishers, and quotations.** None can be produced
  honestly from recall, so none appear.
- **Journal articles.** The `article` type is in the schema and no article is
  seeded, because article citations need volume, issue, and page details that
  cannot be recalled reliably. Several chapters will depend on article
  literature; finding it is real work, and see QX-4 in
  `/research/QUESTIONS.md`.
- **Archival and newspaper sources.** These need a repository or a database and
  a real search, not a guess.
- **Non-English scholarship**, except where a standard translation exists.
  Chapters 14, 16, 19, and 20 are weakest here, and that weakness is a finding
  to be stated in those chapters rather than concealed.

Marking a source `read: y` when it was skimmed is the single easiest way to
poison this project.

## Gaps this list does not cover

Filling these is research work, not recall work:

- **Chapter 11** has no source here connecting the painting to the machines
  thread, because that connection is currently an assumption. See Q11-6.
- **Chapter 14** has no Indian-authored scholarship on the famine beyond one
  retrospective primary source. See Q14-6.
- **Chapter 16** rests entirely on English-language work. See Q16-5.
- **Chapter 20** has no Afghan-side source at all. See Q20-5.
- **Chapters 09, 17, and 18** need the words of the people the events happened
  to, and those are archival, not bibliographic. See Q09-5, Q17-6, Q18-6.
- **No newspaper source is listed** for any chapter, though the book's argument
  about what contemporaries noticed depends on the press. See QX-5.
- **Q10-3 and Q20-4 have nothing behind them at all**, and they are two of the
  four questions that decide whether the money thread leaves America. Q10-3
  wants the Vienna and Berlin events of 1873 treated as part of the same
  crisis as the American panic; the one chapter 10 entry here,
  `wicker-banking-panics`, carries an American period label in its title.
  Q20-4 wants war finance — whether the Afghan war was paid for from the
  British exchequer or from Indian revenues — and all three chapter 20 entries
  are military or narrative history. So the two questions that would carry the
  money thread furthest from America are the two with no source. Finding them
  is prerequisite work for a structural decision rather than
  detail-gathering, and it should start early enough that a null result is
  still actionable. See `/notes/question-triage.md`.
